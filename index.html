About this project

Landmark Journeys

An interactive atlas of journeys that shaped history across eight Earth domains, with spaceflight trajectories on the 3D globe.

AI-generated project · not a historical source

Most of this site—including its descriptions and route reconstructions—was created with AI assistance. The paths are approximate interpretations of available information, not recorded tracks. Details, coordinates, and even source interpretations may be incomplete or wrong. Do not cite this site as a source; check the linked original sources and independent references for research or publication. Do not use these routes for navigation.

Route geometry and uncertainty

Most flights predate continuous position recording. The map connects researched endpoints, stops, landfalls, and logged positions. Two-point routes use great-circle arcs; multi-point routes use a spherical spline through every anchor.

Dense vertices make the lines smooth—not more historically certain. All domains interpolate between researched anchors. Solid strokes mark better-anchored courses; dashes mark inferred connections or low-confidence routes. Neither means an observed track. Each card states its evidence and limits.

Ocean-liner curves follow illustrative sea corridors and are checked against a coarse land mask. Generalized harbors, estuaries, small islands, and historical course changes remain unresolved; the lines are not navigational routes.

Research sources

Cards link to their principal and segment sources. A link does not establish that every claim or point on the drawn line is verified. Where a matching English Wikipedia article has been verified, its icon appears at the top right of the card. Sources include NASA, the NPS, NTSB, Smithsonian museums, national archives, manufacturers, and other institutional histories.

Open researched route anchors in any card to see the named points used to construct its track.

Maps and photographs

Satellite imagery is served by Esri and contributors. Atlas, night, and minimal maps use bundled Natural Earth country data; live attribution appears at the lower-right of the map.

Every journey card includes a sourced image: aircraft, vessels, trains, vehicles, expeditions, portraits, maps, or route settings. Each card links its image source and reuse terms; captions identify later depictions, replicas, museum displays, and representative scenes. Source images are resized, padded, and converted to WebP for display.

Data and access

Each route can be downloaded as WKT or GeoJSON in CRS84. The atlas is a static, client-side site with no account. Cloudflare Web Analytics measures site traffic; satellite mode requests map tiles from Esri. The Wikipedia icon is the Wikimedia project's W favicon, linked to its source and terms below.

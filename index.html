About this project

Landmark Journeys

An interactive atlas of 200 routes that shaped history: 25 flights and 25 journeys in each of seven other domains.

Route geometry and uncertainty

Most flights predate continuous position recording. The map connects researched endpoints, stops, landfalls, and logged positions. Two-point routes use great-circle arcs; multi-point routes use a spherical spline through every anchor.

Dense vertices make the lines smooth—not more historically certain. All domains interpolate between researched anchors. Solid strokes mark better-anchored courses; dashes mark inferred connections or low-confidence routes. Neither means an observed track. Each card states its evidence and limits.

Research sources

Flight cards link to their principal source and a specific Wikipedia article. The other domains link to segment sources and source-backed research briefs. Sources include NASA, the NPS, NTSB, Smithsonian museums, national archives, manufacturers, and other institutional histories.

Open researched route anchors in any card to see the named points used to construct its track.

Maps and photographs

Satellite imagery is served by Esri and contributors. Atlas, night, and minimal maps use bundled Natural Earth country data; live attribution appears at the lower-right of the map.

The 25 aircraft images comprise 11 public-domain, 7 CC0, 3 CC BY 2.0, 3 CC BY-SA 2.0, and 1 CC BY-SA 3.0 works. Each image carries its own credit and license link.

Data and access

Each route can be downloaded as WKT or GeoJSON in CRS84. The atlas is a static, client-side site with no account or analytics; satellite mode requests map tiles from Esri.

No blanket license replaces the terms attached to third-party maps or images. Follow the linked source and license for reuse.

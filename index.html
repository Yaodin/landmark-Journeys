About this project

Landmark Flight Atlas

An interactive atlas of 25 aviation flights selected for their historical impact, technical achievement, or enduring public significance.

Routes and uncertainty

Most flights predate continuous position recording. The map connects researched endpoints, stops, landfalls, and logged positions. Two-point routes use great-circle arcs; multi-point routes use a spherical spline through every anchor.

Dense vertices make the lines smooth—not more historically certain. Every flight card states its evidence class and the limits of its reconstruction.

Research sources

Flight cards link to their principal source and a specific Wikipedia article. Sources include NASA, the NPS, NTSB, Smithsonian museums, national archives, manufacturers, and other institutional histories.

Open researched route anchors in any card to see the named points used to construct its track.

Maps and photographs

Satellite imagery is served by Esri and contributors. Atlas, night, and minimal maps use bundled Natural Earth country data; live attribution appears at the lower-right of the map.

The 25 aircraft images comprise 11 public-domain, 7 CC0, 3 CC BY 2.0, 3 CC BY-SA 2.0, and 1 CC BY-SA 3.0 works. Each image carries its own credit and license link.

Data and access

Each flight can be downloaded as WKT or GeoJSON in CRS84. The atlas is a static, client-side site with no account or analytics; satellite mode requests map tiles from Esri.

No blanket license replaces the terms attached to third-party maps or images. Follow the linked source and license for reuse.

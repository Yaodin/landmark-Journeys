A note on accuracy

Each journey is researched, with sources linked in its route card. Where possible, we combine maps, logs, and historical accounts; many lines interpolate between known points rather than follow a recorded track. Much of the atlas was created with generative AI, so its routes and descriptions may or may not be accurate.

For entertainment only. The lines are approximate interpretations, not verified tracks. Do not use them for navigation or as a historical source; check the linked sources when accuracy matters.
About this project

Landmark Journeys

An interactive atlas of journeys that shaped history across nine domains, including three-dimensional spaceflight trajectories.

AI-generated project · not a historical source

Most of this site—including its descriptions and route reconstructions—was created with AI assistance. The paths are approximate interpretations of available information, not recorded tracks. Details, coordinates, and even source interpretations may be incomplete or wrong. Do not cite this site as a source; check the linked original sources and independent references for research or publication. Do not use these routes for navigation.

Route geometry and uncertainty

Earth routes connect researched endpoints, stops, landfalls, and logged positions. Flight previews use their full interpolated 3D geometry. Ocean-liner curves follow illustrative sea corridors checked against a coarse land mask; harbors, estuaries, and small islands remain unresolved. Dense vertices make lines smooth, not more historically certain.

Space missions use Earth-centered inertial coordinates rotated at the mission start for a static globe. Deep-space distances are visually compressed beyond 500,000 km; original data is available for download. Some trajectories are sampled ephemerides, others educational reconstructions. No route is suitable for navigation.

Research sources

Cards link to principal and segment sources. A link does not establish that every claim or point on the drawn line is verified. Where available, the Wikipedia icon leads to a matching English article.

Open the researched route anchors in a card to see the named points used to construct its track.

Maps and photographs

Satellite imagery is served by Esri and contributors; CesiumJS provides the globe. Credits appear on the map.

Each journey image links to its source and reuse terms. Later depictions, replicas, museum displays, and representative scenes are identified in the image catalog.

Data and access

Earth routes can be downloaded as WKT or GeoJSON in CRS84. Space paths can be downloaded as time-tagged Earth-centered ICRF Cartesian JSON. This prototype is a static, client-side site; it requires an internet connection for CesiumJS and map imagery.

No blanket license replaces the terms attached to third-party maps or images. Follow the linked source and license for reuse.

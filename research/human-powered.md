# Human powered journeys: 25 atlas candidates

Research shortlist, ranked for recognition, route evidence, and variety of walking, skiing, cycling, climbing, paddling, and rowing. “High” means a known trail, climbing line, or coastal itinerary; “Medium” means recoverable waypoints; “Low” means significant route uncertainty. These labels describe map geometry, not the legitimacy of the achievement. Even high-confidence historical routes are not GPS traces.

## Editorial and mapping cautions

- Label each segment by mode. In around-the-world cycling journeys, ship or air transfers are gaps rather than bicycle track. Some ocean crossings in human-powered circumnavigations used rowing or pedal boats, which should be shown as such.
- Coastlines, rivers, and mountaineering routes can guide a reconstruction, but they are not the actual historical track. Ocean rows in particular need voyage logs or GPS before drawing anything more precise than a broad corridor through waypoints.
- Respect source qualifications: the Appalachian Trail Conservancy describes Shaffer’s achievement as the first *reported* thru-hike; Guinness notes that Outen was towed near Adak and Aston received resupply.
- The Appalachian Trail’s Georgia terminus was Mount Oglethorpe for both Shaffer in 1948 and Gatewood in 1955, not Springer Mountain. The [Smithsonian’s Shaffer exhibit](https://amhistory.si.edu/documentsgallery/exhibitions/appalachian_trail_2.html) and a [contemporary 1955 ATC report](https://appalachiantrail.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/Trail_Years.pdf) identify their starts; [ATC dates the relocation to 1958](https://journeys.appalachiantrail.org/issue/fall-2025/the-next-100-years/). The summit coordinate is an approximate modern geographic reference, not a recovered 1948 or 1955 trail survey.
- Strayed did not walk the High Sierra between Trail Pass/Horseshoe Meadows and Sierra City. [Her companion's first-person account published by PCTA](https://wild.pcta.org/2015/01/28/greg-first-person-story-wild/) says they took a bus to Sierra City because of snow. The atlas leaves that transport transfer unconnected; its two walking lines remain corridor sketches, not digitized 1995 PCT alignment.
- Savage's [first-person Pacific voyage archive](https://www.rozsavage.com/voyages/pacific/) and linked day-by-day posts report GPS positions for all three rowing stages. The canonical anchors now include selected observed fixes with an individual diary URL at each fix, including the turn north toward Tarawa and the approach through the Solomon Sea. Coordinates in degrees and decimal minutes were converted to decimal degrees; connecting lines remain interpolation, not a continuous GPS log. Her [Stage 3 account](https://www.rozsavage.com/voyages/pacific/pacific-ocean-stage-three/) establishes the Madang landing, and [NGA Sailing Directions](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D5-PURL-gpo10258/pdf/GOVPUB-D5-PURL-gpo10258.pdf) supplies an approximate Madang Harbor reference coordinate.
- The Caffyn and Hoffmeister coastal lines previously crossed the Australian mainland between Cape Wessel and Darwin, Cape Leeuwin and Albany, and Shark Bay and Perth. Inferred offshore controls at Cape Don, Walpole, Steep Point, Kalbarri, and Jurien Bay now keep these broad corridor segments on water. The [Northern Territory official gazetteer](https://www.ntlis.nt.gov.au/placenames/print_extract.jsp?id=11542) locates Cape Don; [Hoffmeister's expedition section archive](https://www.freyahoffmeister.com/expeditions/australia-2009/pictures-bilder-oz/aus-sections) verifies the broad Nhulunbuy–Darwin and Kalbarri–Perth sections. None of the added offshore coordinates is an observed kayak fix or claimed landing. A Natural Earth 110m crossing audit reduced the number of ≥10-km inland sea-leg crossings across the human-powered domain from 32 to 26, but remaining coarse-coastline or genuine corridor errors still require research.
- For polar and high-altitude travel, a world slippy map can present the journey’s geography, but altitude and steepness need an inset or elevation profile. Do not interpolate a mountain route across impassable terrain.

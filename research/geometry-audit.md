Six failed the minimum standard of a defensible route corridor and were removed, then six new journeys with documented corridors were added. The atlas again has 200 active routes. Principal source URLs and the route-specific confidence/uncertainty notes are inventoried below; segment/additional sources are in canonical JSON.

### Removed after route verification

- `monte-carlo-rally-1911`: the inaugural rally and Paris-to-Monaco winner are documented by the [Automobile Club de Monaco](https://acm.mc/en/automobile-club/the-club/lautomobile-club-de-monaco/history/), but the mapped intermediate towns were not verified as Rougier's route.
- `coronation-safari-1953`: the line followed an apparently abandoned Lake Victoria proposal, not a verified final course; [event history](https://www.eastafricansafarirally.com/our-history) does not establish its control sequence.
- `de-soto-expedition`: the expedition is documented, but [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/liri/learn/historyculture/de-soto-expedition-1539-1542.htm) describes disputed alignments; the mapped inland points were generic regional guesses.
- `cabeza-de-vaca-traverse`: the crossing is documented, but competing paths through Texas and northern Mexico could not support the depicted inland waypoint chain; see the [Library of Congress discussion](https://blogs.loc.gov/international-collections/2019/10/pas-por-aqui-the-hispanic-heritage-in-the-united-states/).
- `coronado-expedition`: [NPS](https://www.nps.gov/coro/learn/historyculture/what-route-did-the-expedition-take.htm) says the complete route is unknown; the depicted Sonora-to-Plains line crossed large uncertainty zones without sufficient route evidence.
- `queen-elizabeth-1940`: the [Clydebank history](https://www.west-dunbarton.gov.uk/leisure-parks-events/museums-and-galleries/collections/shipbuilding/shipbuilding-clydebank/the-three-queens) supports the secret Clyde-to-New-York crossing, but no source located for its depicted offshore waypoints or wartime zigzags.

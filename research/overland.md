# Overland journeys: 25 atlas candidates

Research shortlist, ranked for recognition, geographic range, and route evidence. These are **candidates**, not ready-to-publish tracks. “High” means a mapped historical alignment or documented itinerary; it does not mean meter-level GPS accuracy. “Medium” means substantial named waypoints; “Low” means the precise path or even some claimed legs remain disputed. Some entries are collective migrations or route networks, as noted.

De Soto, Cabeza de Vaca, and Coronado remain historical research candidates, but were removed from the atlas because their illustrated inland waypoint chains could not be verified. The active 25 instead include the [Donner Party itinerary](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/donner-map/), [Klondike Chilkoot route](https://home.nps.gov/klgo/learn/historyculture/historic-chilkoot-trail.htm), and [Fuchs–Hillary Antarctic traverse](https://alp.lib.sun.ac.za/bitstream/handle/123456789/7474/the_beginning_1_taking_part_in_the_trans_antarctic_expedition.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1). This table preserves the original candidate shortlist; the canonical active selections are in `data/journeys/overland.json` and the [geometry audit](geometry-audit.md).

## Editorial and mapping cautions

- Record `geometry_type` for each feature: documented track, reconstructed path, corridor, or waypoints only. Smooth visual curves must not imply measured positions.
- Treat collective migrations and relay networks as distinct from individual expeditions. The Cherokee removal has multiple routes, separate groups, and forced travel; do not collapse it into one generic “Trail of Tears” line. The [NPS map supplement](https://www.nps.gov/trte/getinvolved/upload/Comprehensive-Management-Plan-Map-Supplement-508.pdf) distinguishes known from uncertain alignment.
- NPS [National Register documentation](https://www.nps.gov/trte/learn/historyculture/upload/National-Register-of-Historic-Places-Multiple-Properties-508.pdf) says eleven detachments took the northern overland route; nine left from camps around Fort Cass and Camp Ross, while two followed the Taylor variant from the Chattanooga area. Red Clay is a Cherokee council site and should not be connected to Fort Cass as though it were the common northern-route departure. The canonical line now begins at Fort Cass, with later place controls still schematic. The [NPS TRTE_NHT GIS layer](https://services1.arcgis.com/fBc8EJBxQRMcHlei/arcgis/rest/services/TRTE_NHT/FeatureServer/0) has separately labeled northern-route segments and individual source-method fields, but a route-network audit is needed before replacing the schematic connectors; designated trail GIS alone does not prove one precise detachment's footsteps.
- Avoid “discovered” language for places already known and inhabited. Identify Indigenous guides, communities, and impacts in the individual descriptions. Explain violence and coercion in entries involving colonial expeditions or displacement.
- Segments traveled by river or ship need their actual mode, or a visible transfer gap. Medieval journeys are broad itinerary reconstructions, not high-resolution route candidates.
- The [NPS Anza campsite mapping report](https://www.nps.gov/juba/learn/historyculture/mapping-historic-campsites-of-the-anza-colonizing-expedition.htm) was built from Anza's and Font's diaries, fieldwork, and historian review. Its online table has 97 rows, one without a coordinate (Querobabi), and labels 57 geocoded sites fixed and 39 hypothesized. The report separately discusses 106 campsites; therefore these 96 plotted coordinates must not be described as a complete inventory or a surveyed trail centerline. The caravan-to-Monterey, Bay reconnaissance, and return are separate segments.
- The Oregon main wagon corridor samples the [NPS OREG_NHT published GIS line](https://services1.arcgis.com/fBc8EJBxQRMcHlei/arcgis/rest/services/OREG_NHT/FeatureServer/0) at roughly 15-km intervals between the existing nine named controls. The service exposes 42 line parts with 20,203 original vertices; the selected route uses 218 anchors, including those controls. Multipart joins used to trace the corridor are short (at most about 55 m), but remain inferred; the GIS describes the approximate designated route at about 1:100,000 scale, not verified emigrant wheel ruts or a single 1840s–60s alignment. The Columbia rafting and Barlow alternatives have not been replaced by this sampled line and remain schematic. This method must be rerun from the authoritative GIS if the source is revised; the sampled anchors are recorded in the canonical journey JSON.

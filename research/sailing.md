# Sailing journeys: 25 atlas candidates

Rank is editorial: recognition, historical impact, geographic range, and route evidence. These are sail-driven sea voyages (including sailing canoes and rafts). **Geometry confidence** describes how securely a route *corridor* can be reconstructed, not whether an exact daily track survives: H = logs/charts or precisely documented legs; M = reliable ports and major waypoints, open-water legs inferred; L = important unresolved route or landfall. Dates are voyage years. Do not present interpolated open-water lines as observed tracks.

**Geometry notes:** For the nineteenth-century and later logs/charts, digitize recorded positions where available. For the 1492 Columbus landfall and Franklin's final movements, show an uncertainty band or contested waypoint. The *Endurance* ice drift, *Kon-Tiki* current drift, and great-cape races need different route construction rules. A smooth curve through ports is not a historical track.
